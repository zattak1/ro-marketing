Why this exists

A Manifesto Against Digital Feudalism


"The Web as I envisaged it, we have not seen yet."  —Tim Berners-Lee

When Tim Berners-Lee envisioned the Web, he imagined a system of and for people—open, free, and powered by millions of independent computers.

Instead, that open network slowly consolidated—and now a small group of tech oligarchs mediate most human interaction online.

They harvest attention with infinite scroll, push-notification loops, FOMO triggers, beautified filters, and artificial dopamine rewards. They sell "connection", but push addiction instead. All terms are negotiable—except maximizing profits.

Years ago, as Facebook Platform developers, we felt the pressure of this system firsthand, long before it became mainstream.
Promises were broken. Algorithms shifted. Apps died overnight. Whatever we built wasn't really ours—it belonged to the platform.
The pain of this experience shed light on the truth hiding in plain sight: power was concentrating, and the open Web was slowly giving way to digital feudalism.

So we set out to build an alternative that preserved the Web's open and free values.

What emerged was the Qbix Platform—an open-source set of tools for communities, not social media, and not a destination for addiction. Really Ours is the first vendor of this platform.

Instead of trying to control people, we hand them the keys…

Build your own features and customize however you wish. Be part of an ecosystem that allows communities to borrow from each other.

This is the moment to step outside the gates. Loneliness is at an all-time high. Disconnection is the new norm.
And every revolution started the same way—with a small group of pioneers willing to take the more difficult path.

What is AGPLv3, and why does it matter?

Copyleft, in plain English

"Copyleft" is a play on "copyright." Where copyright says "you can't copy this without permission," copyleft says "you can copy and modify this freely — as long as you keep it open and let everyone else do the same."

It's a self-perpetuating commitment to openness. Anyone who builds on copyleft code is legally required to release their additions under the same terms. No private forks. No closed-source extensions. No quiet license switches.

The spectrum of openness

  • Permissive (MIT, Apache): "Use it however you want — including closing it back up." Friendly to corporations, vulnerable to extraction.
  • Weak copyleft (GPLv2, LGPL): "Share modifications — but only when you distribute the software as a download." A loophole that SaaS companies routinely exploit.
  • Strong copyleft (AGPLv3, GPLv3): Closes that loophole. Anyone running it as a service — including cloud hosts — has to share modifications.

Where every community platform sits

PlatformLicenseWhat it means
Mighty Networks, Circle, Skool, Heartbeat, Bettermode Proprietary Closed source. You have no rights to the code. Can't audit, fork, or escape.
Ghost MIT (permissive) Open — but anyone can fork it and make it proprietary. No protection.
WordPress + BuddyPress, Discourse GPLv2 (weak copyleft) Open — improvements must be shared if software is distributed. SaaS hosts can keep their changes private.
Qbix Platform (Really Ours), Mastodon, HumHub, Forem AGPLv3 (strong copyleft) Open — improvements must be shared even by cloud hosts. The strongest commitment to the commons.

Why we picked it

Every successful open-source platform that grew popular has been pressured to weaken its license — Elasticsearch, Redis, MongoDB, and Terraform all "rug-pulled" once cloud providers built competing services on their code. They could only do it because their licenses left room.

AGPLv3 doesn't. Once code ships under it, no one can quietly reel it back in — not us, not future owners, not cloud hosts, not investors. The code Qbix releases today stays open, forkable, and yours forever.

That's the point.
